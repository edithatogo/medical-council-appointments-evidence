Background and context
1.      You will be meeting with the Chair and Chief Executive of the Pharmacy Council (the
Council) on 5 June 2025.
Summary of organisation and attendees
2.      The Council is one of 18 responsible authorities (RAs), appointed under section 114 of
the Health Practitioners Competence Assurance Act 2003 (the HPCA Act), that regulates
health practitioners.
3.      The Council regulates pharmacists in New Zealand and is responsible for their
registration, setting their scope of practice, standards for qualification, accrediting
training programmes, and reviewing pharmacists when their competence or professional
4.
conduct is a concern.
The Council consists of eight members, who are appointed for a three-year term.
Members can be appointed for a total of up to nine consecutive years. You will be
meeting with the Chair of the Council and the Chief Executive.
a)
Ming-chun Wu, Chair: Ming-chun is the recently elected Chair of the Council. She
has been a Council member for nearly six years and has a strong background in
governance and public policy. She has held senior strategy and policy roles in large
public sector organisations and has in-depth knowledge of governing legislation.
Ming-chun is also a board member and Deputy Chair of the Chinese Medicine
Council of New Zealand and a board member of the Medical Council of New
Zealand, where she contributes her expertise in regulatory oversight, professional
standards, and governance.
b)
Michael Pead, Chief Executive: Michael has held the Chief Executive role for nine
years. He has maintained a focus on public interest regulation. His professional
background is in public policy reform and regulatory leadership. He has experience
in public sector reform, policy development (infrastructure, housing, and education
policy development in particular), regulatory design and application, and
implementation and operationalisation of policy. Michael has held senior roles at
NZ Treasury, the Department of Prime Minister and Cabinet, and Ernst & Young
consulting.
Topics for discussion
5.      The Council has indicated they are interested in discussing the following topics:
Registration of overseas-trained pharmacists
6.      Ministry officials have been encouraging RAs to review their registration processes for
PR      overseas-trained practitioners with a view to ensuring they are as efficient as possible.
We acknowledge that RAs must also ensure that a thorough assessment of skills and
qualifications is completed to ensure registrants are fit and competent to practise in the
New Zealand context.
7.      Each year roughly 10-15% of newly registered pharmacists are overseas-trained. The
Council has implemented a fast-track process for pharmacists from countries with similar
pharmacy practice and qualification to New Zealand. In response to pharmacy sector

Supporting prescribing pharmacists
10.
The Council is enhancing its regulatory framework for pharmacist prescribing to ensure
that it enables rather than restricts the contributions that pharmacist prescribers can
11.
make to the health system.
The Pharmacist Prescriber scope of practice authorises advanced clinical pharmacists to
prescribe certain prescription medicines. Entry and ongoing requirements (including
minimum practice experience) have been set to reflect the advanced level of practice
needed to safely prescribe autonomously.
12.
Recent funding to support the primary care workforce has attracted more pharmacists to
complete the qualification to register in the Pharmacist Prescriber scope of practice,
which prompted Pharmacy Council to review the entry requirements and conduct a
survey of current pharmacist prescribers, followed by a focus group.
13.
The review supported maintaining the current registration requirements for pharmacist
prescribers. It also supported an exemption from monitoring requirements for
experienced prescribers as well as a scaffolded approach to supporting new registrants
who do not fully meet the registration requirements. This would allow new registrants to
practise in a more limited scope, with conditions to manage future expansion of their
prescribing practice.
14.
The Council may wish to discuss other factors impacting the safety, uptake, and efficacy
of pharmacist prescribing within New Zealand. This includes ensuring that less
experienced pharmacist prescribers have adequate professional and work-based support
to safely manage expansion of their prescribing practice, employer recognition of the
value of pharmacist prescribers, and improved sector understanding of the pharmacist
prescriber role.
Supporting innovation
15.     The Council has started work on a draft position statement that will aim to support
O      innovative practice for pharmacists and pharmacist prescribers. This statement will
acknowledge and support the expanding role of pharmacists and pharmacist prescribers
16.
in meeting the growing healthcare needs of New Zealanders.
The Council is currently forming a working party to develop this position statement. One
of the aims of the statement is to bring awareness of the broad and extensive nature of
the existing scopes of practice for pharmacists and pharmacist prescribers, and how they
can be leveraged to deliver innovative and flexible services.

Other topics or issues that may arise
Review of the HPCA Act
17.     The Ministry has engaged closely with the Pharmacy Council, along with other RAs,
throughout the review of the HPCA Act. The Council’s submission was in favour of more
patient involvement in decisions, and they were interested in shared services and
functions for regulators, and joint standards. They were open to new pathways to
registration and new professions and regulatory options, but saw identifying them as a
role for commissioners, rather than regulators.
Changes to prescribing lengths
18.
The extended maximum prescription length from 3 months to 12 months, expected to
come into effect in early 2026, will mean people with long-term or chronic conditions
may be able to continue accessing their medicines for longer periods of time between
appointments with their prescriber.
19.
Medicines will continue to be dispensed from pharmacies in intervals of up to a
maximum of three months.
20.
Patients will continue to be charged the same prescription co-payment for medicines as
they are now, which for most patients is $5 per medicine per prescription. There are no
changes to eligibility for prescription fee exemptions or the prescription subsidy scheme.
Patients on longer prescriptions will be charged the $5 prescription co-payment every 3
21.
months when they pick up their medicines.
Next steps include amendment to the Medicines Regulations (the Ministry), changes to
the Pharmaceutical Schedule (Pharmac), and updating the electronic systems for
prescribing and dispensing of medicines (Health NZ).
Expansion of prescribing rights
22.
The Medicines Amendment Bill, currently in Select Committee, is proposing to allow
authorised prescribers to prescribe funded alternative unapproved medicines in stock
shortage situations.
23.
The Pharmacy Council has made a submission, supporting the proposal on the grounds
of flexibility and putting patients at the centre.
