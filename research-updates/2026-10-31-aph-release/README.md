The September release contains an APH appointments paper. The missing records remain the actual APH-26-MIN-0074 minute and the reasons for Love’s non-progression. References to the minute in later briefings are not the minute. The paper itself should not be described as missing, and its supposed nonpublication should not be used to imply concealment.

March correspondence(G045Document4,PDF27–28) explicitly describes delays and additional candidates requiring checks. It suggests30JuneAPH; the actual12Maymeeting was earlier. No missed30Junedeadline or causal link between reversal and delay is inferred. The late change after selection strengthens the governance case for explaining what changed. Crown Law’s Q22guidance supports recording reasons where recommendations are not accepted.

Absence of reasons from the public release does not establish whether reasons were withheld, omitted, retained elsewhere or inadequately recorded. It does not prove deliberate evasion. Cabinet Manual8.30 also recognises that existing information can be official information even if unwritten. Recordkeeping and public disclosure are related but distinct questions.
